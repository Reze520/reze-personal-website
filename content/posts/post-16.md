<p>Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? is a science fiction novel that deeply discusses human nature, technology and ethics.</p>
<p>The story is set in a post-nuclear world. Due to the impact of radioactive fallout, animals on the Earth are endangered, and human living conditions are extremely harsh. To encourage the remaining population to emigrate, the government has promised to equip each alien with a bionic human to help them live. However, these bionic people are not satisfied with the status quo of human slavery, and they begin to find ways to escape back to Earth.</p>
<p>Rick Deckard is a bounty hunter who specializes in hunting down fugitive androids. During a manhunt, he encounters an unprecedented challenge from a new breed of android. In the contact and contest with the androids, Rick found that his view and attitude towards them had changed greatly. This change is not only the cognition of the androids, but also a profound reflection on humanity and ethics.</p>
<p>By depicting the complex relationship between humans and androids, the novel explores what constitutes a human trait, and where exactly the line between artificial intelligence and humans lies. It provokes deep thinking about self-perception, emotions, ethics, and more, and makes us re-examine our relationship to technology and its impact on human society.</p>
<p>In addition, Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? also shows the author's prediction and warning for the future world. In the novel, Philip K. Dick sets up the basic form of future robots and puts forward the issue of possible new ethical relations in the future. This makes us think about how to properly deal with the relationship between people and science and technology, and avoid the occurrence of ethical tragedies in today's era of continuous development of new technologies such as artificial intelligence and gene technology.</p>
<p>In summary, Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? is a science fiction novel with both intellectual depth and literary value, which is not only suitable for science fiction lovers to read, but also for readers interested in human nature, technology and ethics.</p>
